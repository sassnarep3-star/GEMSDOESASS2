Why GEMSDOE32’s 0.2778 Scored Highest — Ph.D.-Level Forensic Analysis

Across the nine focus sites, the organizing factor is not raw dot count but off-catalogue novelty under the distance-weighted Tversky kernel (α=0.2 β=0.8, 300 m triangular). Every high score above 0.27 enforces a hard catalogue buffer.

  • What H33-2-B2 actually does (verified from audit JSON): It starts from the 0.2708 base (h27-4-solo, 40,199 dots) and deletes every dot with d(catalogue) ≤ 2 px (200 m), leaving 37,654 dots with 0 on-catalogue and 0 within 200 m. Tversky with β=0.8 heavily penalizes false negatives near hidden faults but only α=0.2 penalizes false positives — so deleting near-catalogue false positives costs little but removes the dominant source of organizer “on-catalogue leakage” that the metric treats as FPw ≈ p·(1−k(d)) ≈ p (since k(d)=1 at d=0). In plain terms: predicting a known fault again gives you no TP credit (it’s already in the training labels) and merely risks a FP; the optimizer learns to move mass off known traces.
  • Density sweet spot: 60k (GEMSDOE24) at B=1.5 scored 0.2477 → too dense, many near-catalogue FP. 44k at B=2.8 scored 0.2600 → better but still not enough pruning. 37.6k at B=2.0 scored 0.2778 → the Pareto optimum found by the Bayesian Optimization surrogate in GEMSDOE32 (docs/assets/fig2_bo_gp_surrogate_and_drift.png). Smaller (38k Anderson) also scores 0.275 — confirming the 37–39k with B≥2 is the ridge top.
  • Why GEMSDOE27’s gap-closure trailed (0.2449): It added 1,259 dots on 1–4 km gap links. The metric’s 300 m kernel means a correctly closed gap gives only a narrow 300 m band of TPw credit, but a false gap closure adds a long linear FPw penalty. The net was negative.
  • Why GEMSDOE25/30’s 44k Poisson still lost: Poisson-disk off-catalogue sampling spreads dots uniformly, but the true hidden faults are not Poisson — they cluster at step-overs and hydrothermal corridors. Uniform spreading wastes budget on barren piedmont.
  • Why GEMSDOE36’s PINN almost tied (0.2750) with totally different physics: Anderson’s PINN encodes heat-flow physics (conduction + advection) and uses geothermal well/spring chemistry — it finds blind conduits that have no topographic expression, i.e., the same off-catalogue niches that H33-2 finds empirically by pruning. Its 0.2750 validates that thermal signal is the underexploited lane.
  • Can we beat 0.2778? Yes, but not by re-tuning B on the same dotted-ridge surface (the GP surrogate already exhausted that family; Arena51 audit shows family Spearman 0.844 with score, and lattice novelty is pigeonholed at 99.87% coverage — you cannot make a 37k ridge variant that is literally 70% novel). The only headroom is a different surface that discovers faults the ridge family misses: hydrothermal vent conduits evidenced by 2 m temperature probes (GDR 1391, 544 probes), K-depletion/ThK hydrothermal alteration (GeoDAWN rad), and blind-basin MT clay-cap edges. Our TTC lane does exactly that: triple-concordance (lidar step ∩ radiometric alteration ∩ thermal residual) at 300 m inhibition gives HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1215 vs 0.0992 for the champion on the same hide-and-recover v3.0 evaluator (paired Δ +0.0223 [0.011,0.031], p≈1.0, 22k withheld) and simultaneously beats champion on off-catalogue SGMC (0.068 vs 0.064) — the only TTC variant to win on both instruments. That paired win is why its projection exceeds 0.2778, but it remains a projection until organizer confirms.
