The reported GEMSDOE32 result

The user-reported historical value 0.2778 is the highest among the nine listed claims, not an authenticated score. Reopening the pinned rasters shows:

  • GEMSDOE32 H33-B2's active support is a subset of GEMSDOE31's B1 support; B1 has 2,545 additional positive pixels and B2 has none outside it.
  • GEMSDOE36's Anderson raster contains B2 and 1,200 additional positive pixels.
  • GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 contain identical active support in this sample footprint, although their TIFF bytes are different.
  • Each selected site file exceeds the 70% directional overlap threshold against at least one of the other eight selected files. They should not be presented as a new independent entry.

The official metric uses a 300 m triangular distance kernel and weights false negatives more heavily than false positives. Removing spatially redundant predictions can lower false-positive mass while retaining nearby true-positive coverage. That is a plausible reason thinning and B2 pruning performed well, not proof: the hidden expert labels and score-to-file receipts are unavailable. Known-fault pixels, not an entire near-fault buffer, are masked according to DrivenData staff. Do not infer that all unknown faults within 200 m of a mapped fault have zero credit. An improvement over the reported historical value is possible, but no local evidence supports a numeric prediction or a claim it will happen.

Independent literal-registry stop

The shared-template gate defines support as finite prediction > 0. The pinned template's own registry includes the 17GEMSDOE continuous surface. We re-read it on the pinned sample grid: it is positive at every finite footprint pixel. Under the literal support rule, any nonempty in-footprint dot set has 100% directional overlap within three pixels, and must stop. Whether continuous surfaces should be treated as dot sets is a real protocol ambiguity; silently exempting this entry would change the shared rule.

Even if that ambiguity were explicitly resolved, the separately registered pinned spacing-five lattice leaves only 6,649 of 5,167,373 sample-footprint cells outside its three-pixel halo. For any N-dot set, at least (N − 6649) / N dots lie inside the halo (when N > 6649). At the historically common 37,654-dot budget the lower bound is 82.34%, so the requested >70% stop is unavoidable. This bound by itself does not prove every smaller candidate fails. Rearranging dots solely to dodge the check would not establish geological discovery or a high score.

Two site claims were rechecked against the actual saved bytes, not adopted as our release policy:

  • GEMSDOE28 S22C: 99.62% overlap against the lattice. Its own run card notes the reported holdout was not buffered; it is not our protocol-cleared candidate.
  • GEMSDOE36 KPL-36: 99.17% overlap against the lattice. Its site promotes it against the nine-site subset; that is not a pass against the literal expanded registry. Its own receipt also separates format from upload approval.

These are overlap measurements, not scores. Their pre-placement surfaces were not retrieved or checked, and no claim of full registry coverage is made. The universal positive-surface witness additionally blocks each under the unchanged literal implementation. See the full hashes and values in the JSON.

Format and the reported range error

The official problem description requests a single-band float32 GeoTIFF, EPSG:32611, 100 m resolution, the same bounds/grid as the training data, and probabilities in [0,1], with null/NaN outside the stated bounds. Exact shape/transform must be verified against the participant's organizer-provided sample. The error message alone does not identify which file, pixel, metadata or outside-footprint encoding caused the rejection.

python scripts/check_geotiff.py FILE --sample SAMPLE reopens bytes, compares the grid, checks finite values, NaN/Inf and [0,1] inside the reference footprint, reports the dataset mask, and invokes the unchanged shared-template validator. That validator imposes a stricter all-finite policy; an official- style NaN-outside file may fail that local policy. Conversely, zero-filled outside-footprint pixels can be all finite but are not marked null by a dataset mask. The official words “outside the bounds” and a nonrectangular footprint, and the portal's treatment of masks/NaN, have not been reconciled. Neither local encoding alone proves portal acceptance. No clipping or silent repair.

The owner-mirrored sample has 60,988 positive pixels, all on the owner-mirrored catalogue, whereas the official page describes a total-absence sample. This is a provenance discrepancy; those bytes must not be called organizer-authenticated.
