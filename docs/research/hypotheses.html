Rank / expected DTI opportunity Layers and signature Why it might find catalogue-missing faults Difference / competing explanation Cost and data readiness
1 · relatively promising, uncertain Provided reduced-to-pole magnetics, isostatic gravity anomaly and detrended elevation. Compare oriented, normalized two-sided magnetic texture matches at several cross-strike offsets, require the inferred offset sign to persist with scale and be consistent with a gravity-side contrast; use elevation only as an independent, nonmandatory corroborator. A fault can displace magnetically contrasting strata even when young cover conceals its surface trace. The candidate must be derived without the withheld catalogue; the visible catalogue is used only as permitted by the fold. More specific than a single magnetic-edge or crest detector: tests a repeated, signed correspondence across the suspected trace, with a scale-stability negative control. Displaced-lag/marker work exists in sibling sites; distinction from every implementation is unverified. Mimics: a meandering depositional/lithological boundary, intrusive contact, survey leveling seam. Medium/high CPU. Layers are described by the official competition page; the required prepared shared cache is not available here. Not currently runnable under the protocol. USGS magnetic-strata study and Dixie Valley case motivate, but do not validate, the test.
2 · moderate, uncertain GeoDAWN potassium, equivalent thorium and magnetics: robust centered log-ratio contrasts on both sides of a magnetic lineament, after conditioning on mapped lithology/cover and measurement coverage. Fault displacement or alteration could generate a compositional boundary under modest cover without a catalogue trace; a field is considered only where all inputs exist. Distinct from a bare radiometric edge by testing flank asymmetry and conditioning on composition and coverage. Other sites have tested radiometric/magnetic fusion; full method exclusivity is not known. Mimics: unfaulted bedrock contacts, sediment sorting, radon disequilibrium and survey seams. Medium/high. USGS GeoDAWN release publicly lists radiometric products and CC0, and ScienceBase lists grids. Listing was accessed; binary files, coverage and units were not fetched or validated in this sandbox. Lithology source and licenses still need checking; data-blocked, not yet viable.
3 · moderate/low, uncertain USGS 3DEP bare-earth elevation plus mapped drainage: seek repeated, similarly oriented cross-valley profile steps or stream offsets; reject steps following contours or mapped infrastructure. Small tectonic scarps on alluvial surfaces may be absent from a coarse or older fault catalogue. Requires multiple independently displaced profiles rather than thresholding one scarp or drainage line; sibling 3DEP/scarp work is extensive and exhaustive novelty is unverified. Mimics: alluvial terraces, shorelines, landslides and roads. High: tile acquisition, co-registration and negative-control masks. USGS 3DEP states its products are free; coverage for the specific area has not been verified, and no binary tiles were retrieved here. Data-blocked.
4 · exploratory, high uncertainty GDR INGENIOUS two-metre temperature probes and spring/well geochemistry, with magnetic/gravity lineaments. Test along-line thermal/geochemical concordance against matched sampling-density and hydrogeology controls. A permeable concealed structural corridor might influence heat and fluid transport where no mapped surface trace exists. Tests a directional multi-site contrast instead of distance to a single vent; prior hydrothermal-conduit approaches exist. Mimics: groundwater mixing and lateral flow, shallow solar heating, and biased survey placement. The target remains a fault, not an automatically discovered vent. High; sparse and biased measurements. GDR 1391 publicly lists downloadable probes, wells and springs under CC BY 4.0; the landing page was verified, but binary packages, applicability and license for each linked dataset were not independently checked. Data-blocked.

Frozen validation plan once blockers are resolved

  1. Receive one explicit method lane; verify it does not conflict with a concurrent registry lane. Freeze the candidate, nuisance controls, dot budget and seeds.
  2. Restore the shared prepared feature cache, not an independently rebuilt one, and use the unmodified shared evaluate_holdout.py and submission_writer.py. Authenticate the official template and labels.
  3. Before placing dots, measure rank correlation and literal support overlap against the complete current registry. Stop at the prescribed threshold.
  4. Hide buffered whole fault segments, rebuild every catalogue-derived feature from the visible catalogue in each fold, mask visible faults pixel-exactly, and evaluate each feature independently for leakage. AUC above 0.90 is a leak alarm, not evidence of performance.
  5. Use pooled distance-weighted Tversky (α 0.2, β 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel) and a paired spatial-block 95% confidence interval against the same evaluator's frozen current best. Catalogue holdout is only a proxy for newly expert-mapped faults; it is not a leaderboard score or guarantee.
  6. If and only if the pre-placement checks pass, place dots, repeat the literal uniqueness test on the final saved raster, run the shared writer and saved-byte validation, and emit an immutable run card. Stop at three experiments or two hours; a later separate selector decides about a slot.

No step above was executed in this preflight. Reinterpreting the dense-surface or lattice witnesses without a protocol change would silently weaken the rule.
