Overview / Research

Hypotheses, not predictions.

Physical mechanisms matter. So do their mimics, data provenance, and the distinction between a catalogue proxy and hidden competition truth.

Forensic finding

What explains the reported 0.2778?

The source’s owner-report attributes 0.2778 to GEMSDOE32 H33-2-B2; an organizer submission receipt linking the exact file to that number is not available. Our independent comparison of pinned owner-hosted variants shows:

  • GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have exactly the same positive pixels inside the mirror footprint.
  • GEMSDOE32 B2 equals that GEMSDOE25 mask after removing 6,436 dots at distance ≤2 pixels from the mirror catalogue; the symmetric difference is zero.
  • GEMSDOE31’s solo mask equals the same parent after removing 3,891 dots at distance ≤1 pixel; B2 removes another 2,545 of those dots.

Under the distance-weighted Tversky metric, a dot far from hidden truth contributes false-positive mass; removing such dots can improve precision. Conversely, it can discard genuine nearby unseen faults. DrivenData staff say the known-fault mask is pixel-exact and new faults can be nearby. We cannot infer the hidden TP/FP/FN terms, explain the observed result causally, or assert that a new unique file will beat it. Read the calculations ↗

Why there is no score estimate yet

A catalogue holdout and the hidden expert-labelled leaderboard are different populations. A defensible projection would require multiple organizer receipts linked to exact raster hashes, the same frozen holdout evaluator for each, and a calibration tested on held-out submission families—not random copies of the same dotted lineage. Compare it against an intercept-only baseline, report calibration error and uncertainty, and reject it under distribution shift. No such calibrated evidence is available here, so the estimate remains unavailable. Even a validated projection would be labeled as a projection, never as a score.

Four directions to investigate

Rank is a qualitative judgment of potential, not a numeric score forecast. None has been run here. This checkout began empty; these operators are unimplemented here. Similar work exists in sibling projects, and absence from the complete registry has not been established. The top proposal is not an assigned parallel-run lane.

01 · potentially high / medium cost

Sensor-perturbation-stable buried boundaries

Layers & signature: the provided reduced-to-pole magnetic field, total magnetic intensity and its gradients, isostatic gravity and basement-depth estimate. Build co-located boundary features that remain stable across fixed, physically justified noise/scale perturbations and whole-modality dropouts; do not use fault labels to set the perturbations.

Why missing faults: a buried displacement may persist in more than one independent physical response even where no mapped scarp exists. This tests perturbation stability, not merely the displaced-lag and throw-covariance features already reported by CSDL-36. Mimic: a persistent lithologic contact or dike swarm can produce stable anomalies too.

Feasibility: requires the reusable competition feature cache and matched holdout, neither present here; the official data are login-gated. The underlying data context is described by USGS GeoDAWN. Novelty across all parallel work is unverified.

02 · potentially medium / medium-high cost

Cover-conditioned radiometric contrast reversals

Layers & signature: GeoDAWN K, Th and U grids plus a verified, non-fault lithology/cover layer. Measure whether a cross-strike radiometric contrast changes sign or persists after conditioning on mapped rock units, rather than using a raw K/Th anomaly as direct fault evidence.

Why missing faults: alteration or offset lithologic packages might betray a permeable or buried structure not in the catalogue. Mimic: sediment sorting, lithological boundaries and radon or moisture effects. Prior GEMSDOE32 work already used radiometric ratios; this proposed conditional contrast is not evidence that the family is new.

Feasibility: the USGS release lists radiometric grids, but this checkout has not downloaded or validated them, a lithology layer, coverage or alignment. Research-only until that happens.

03 · potentially medium / high cost

Absolute-elevation shoreline counterfactual

Layers & signature: USGS 3DEP absolute elevation and terrain profiles, combined with 100 m topography. Compare signed scarp-profile persistence inside and outside independently mapped paleoshoreline bands; do not use detrended elevation as absolute elevation.

Why missing faults: weak basin-margin steps may continue beneath cover where catalogues are incomplete; explicit counterfactual checks may suppress lake-level landforms. Mimic: wave-cut benches, fan terraces, and road cuts. This is a shoreline-confound test rather than merely another scarp score.

Feasibility: USGS says 3DEP products are free and unrestricted; the necessary tiles and shoreline coverage have not been retrieved or checked here. Not yet viable for validation.

04 · uncertain / high cost

Acquisition-level magnetic reproducibility

Layers & signature: GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line and tie-line CSVs, flight-path geometry and terrain clearance. Test whether directional profile breaks recur across adjacent flight lines and remain consistent at crossovers, accounting for line levelling and survey geometry.

Why missing faults: reproducible subsurface breaks might be obscured in gridded maps or lack a surface expression. Mimic: line levelling, flight-height changes, dike swarms and ordinary contacts. It uses acquisition-level checks, unlike grid-only models, but other sibling reports have already proposed raw-profile methods.

Feasibility: the official USGS release lists flight-line CSV archives and flight paths. Direct archive access and study-area coverage were not verified from this network-restricted execution environment; not yet viable for a run.

From geothermal system to fault target

The competition predicts fault locations, not vents directly. The USGS Great Basin play-fairway report describes integrating geology, geophysics and geochemistry, including a blind system with no surface hot springs, fumaroles or paleogeothermal deposits. It motivates multimodal hypotheses; it does not establish their performance on this challenge. The Granite Springs study illustrates that magnetic highs can also reflect an inferred dike swarm or lithologic contact.
