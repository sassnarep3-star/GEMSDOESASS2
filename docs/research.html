Four prospective XGHF-family tests

Qualitative rank and cost are judgments, not score forecasts. No experiment was run in this session; novelty across all sibling repositories is unverified. The prompt did not supply the referenced assigned lane paragraph.

01 · potentially high / medium cost

Multiscale sensor-perturbation stability

Layers and transform: reduced-to-pole magnetics, isostatic gravity and their gradients; compare cross-gradient boundary orientation over multiple smoothing scales and leave-one-modality-out perturbations.

Why hidden faults: displaced density and magnetization contacts may persist beneath alluvium. Mimic: lithologic contacts or dikes. Existing XGHF has pointwise cross-gradients; this tests perturbation stability, which was not found in a local code search. The 19-band owner mirror was restored, not organizer-authenticated.

02 · potentially medium / medium cost

Cover-conditioned boundary disagreement

Layers and transform: magnetic and gravity gradient normals with basement-depth and detrended-slope proxies; test local directional disagreement by cover regime. Why: field contrasts may reveal buried structure without a mapped scarp. Mimic: stratigraphic or intrusive margins. This conditional interaction differs from a raw cross-gradient; untested.

03 · potentially medium / medium cost

Sampling-aware hydrothermal evidence

Layers and transform: potential-field boundaries, spring/well or 2-m temperature observations, and an independent survey-effort background. Why: an upflow corridor could mark concealed permeability. Mimic: preferential sampling around known prospects or warm groundwater without a fault. Existing code uses hydrothermal plumes; this proposed sampling correction is not apparent in the local code. GDR 1391 publicly lists the datasets, but actual archives and survey-effort coverage were not retrieved here; not yet viable.

04 · potential low–medium / low–medium cost

Visible-tip and field-boundary angular residual

Layers and transform: fold-visible fault-tip tangents and local magnetic/gravity boundary orientations; compare angular residuals against a tip-only baseline. Why: a blind splay may extend a mapped structure into a field boundary. Mimic: erosional lineaments or survey-line artifacts. This interaction is proposed, not established by the existing fracture fields.
