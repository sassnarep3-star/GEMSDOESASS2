Five hypotheses, ranked — and which ones are already dead

Each entry names the layers, the physical signature, why it should catch a fault missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue rather than one already in it, how it differs from anything implemented here, the non-fault process that could mimic it, and the official free source. Ranked by expected DTI gain per unit cost. None may reach a submission slot before it beats the champion on a buffered spatial holdout.

Status note. This page is a research register from the 2026-10-31 H-CORRIDOR lane. That lane's own file is a measured, non-promoted candidate — see the README and IR-31-08. The one file cleared to submit is unchanged.

What this session measured first, and why it changes the ranking. Three things: (i) no proxy truth set predicts the leaderboard (40 rasters, all LOO R² negative); (ii) the champion's lineage is genuinely geophysically informed — its dots sit at 1.80× the mean model probability of random cells; (iii) a gradient-boosted model fitted on the public catalogue reaches only 0.6975 spatially-blocked AUC, and placing cells by it does not beat the champion on the proxy. Any new hypothesis must therefore supply information the 56-band stack does not already contain — which is why the thermal and hydrologic fields rank at the top.

H10 · Shallow (2 m) temperature-probe residual field RANK 1 · untried · cost low

Layers. UNR/GBCGE INGENIOUS 2-metre shallow temperature-probe regional survey, DOE GDR submission 1391, DOI 10.15121/1881483, CC BY 4.0. Local mirror verified present: 2m_temperature_probe_INGENIOUS_regional_data.zip and the rasterised derived_gdr_2m_probes_100m_u8.tif (both hash-pinned in registry/data_manifest.json).

Physical signature. Advective heat transport: a permeable fault carrying 60–120 °C water to within a few metres of the surface raises the 2 m ground temperature above the conductive baseline. The target is the residual after removing the elevation and latitude trend, not the raw temperature — the trend is dominated by climate, the residual by upflow.

Why it catches a fault the catalogue misses. The USGS Quaternary catalogue is scarp-dependent: no scarp, no entry. A concealed or bedrock fault with active upflow has no scarp at all but does have a thermal halo. This is the only field in the stack that measures the geothermal property the competition is named for, rather than a structural proxy for it.

How it differs from this repository. The existing register lists H4 (probe-temperature kriging) as untried and this session confirms nothing here reads the probe values. The 56 bands the H-CORRIDOR candidate was built from contain no temperature channel: they are magnetics, gravity, strain, seismicity, conductivity, elevation and radiometrics.

Non-fault mimic. Shallow water table, irrigated pasture, pavement and playa albedo all shift 2 m temperature. Mitigation: require the residual to be elongated (structure-tensor coherence of the residual field) and cross-cutting to the regional gradient, and require concurrence with a second independent edge.

Data status. Free, official, CC BY 4.0 (share-alike-compatible with the sponsor requirement), and verified obtainable — mirrored in a public GEMS repository and already rasterised onto the competition grid.

H13 · Upflow triple intersection — seismicity × conductivity-depth × dilatation RANK 2 · untried · cost low

Layers. Competition bands 16 (ieq_n100a15 earthquake density), 17 (cond_surf conductivity) and 15 (depth_to_base_surf), plus band 8 (geod_dilaterate). No external data.

Physical signature. A convective geothermal cell concentrates microseismicity above the upflow, caps with a conductive clay layer at a shallow depth, and sits in a dilatational (opening) quadrant where permeability is maintained. The signature is the intersection, not any single field.

Why it catches a fault the catalogue misses. Each of the three fields is individually weak on this grid (univariate AUC 0.44–0.58 measured this session), which is precisely why no one has built on them; the joint restriction to cells that are simultaneously seismically active, shallow-clay-capped and dilatational is a much smaller set and is not represented in the scarp-based catalogue.

How it differs from this repository. The HVFD lane used hydro-features separately; the current model uses them as three of 68 weak features in a global fit. A multiplicative intersection prior, applied as a separate emission channel, has never been tested on the holdout.

Non-fault mimic. Reservoir-induced seismicity, aquifer drawdown and tectonic swarms; conductive clay basins that are purely sedimentary. Mitigation: subtract the basin-depth trend before intersecting.

Data status. In-repo, no external dependency.

H8 · Corridor emission geometry (metric-mechanical, not geological) RANK 3 · tested this session · negative

Layers. None new — the same probability surface, different shape.

Physical signature. Under the official metric a pixel emitted on a fault earns credit from every truth pixel within 300 m (roughly five of them along a thin trace) and pays a false-positive cost of 1 − k(d) that falls to zero as the line approaches the trace. A space-filling lattice of isolated dots spaced ~1.2 km apart — which is what all nine focus sites are — forfeits that multiplier.

Measured result. Mass-matched corridor emissions (support 51,674 → 1,033,475 px at Σp = 37,654) scored 0.0100–0.0135 on the proxy against the champion's 0.0947 — far worse, not better. The reason is visible in the arithmetic: spreading the same mass over 27× more pixels pays 27× more false-positive distance penalty than it recovers. Negative result, published as a deliverable.

Why it is still ranked. The instrument it failed on has no demonstrated validity (section 2 of the executive summary). It should be re-tested the moment an organizer receipt exists. Cost of a re-test: minutes.

H11 · Tilt-depth consistency along strike (source-parameter imaging) RANK 4 · untried here · cost medium

Layers. GeoDAWN rtp, tmi, tc (tilt angle) and TMI_up150 (upward-continued 150 m, in the extensions raster), plus competition band 15 (depth_to_base_surf).

Physical signature. A fault is a sub-vertical tabular source: the depth estimated from the local wavenumber of the analytic signal should be constant along strike and the source should be steeply dipping. A lithologic contact is a sub-horizontal or variable-depth boundary whose depth estimate wanders along strike. The signature is the variance of the depth estimate along a candidate lineament, not the depth itself.

Why it catches a fault the catalogue misses. It discriminates structure from lithology using geometry alone, which is exactly the confusion that keeps radiometric and magnetic amplitude methods at AUC 0.44–0.58 here.

How it differs from this repository. The register's H2 (magnetic tilt zero-convergence) was tested and is negative (ridge alone 0.0396 vs 0.0489 uniform). This is a different observable: depth-estimate stability along strike, not the zero-crossing of the tilt. Other GEMSDOE sites explored Euler depth clustering (reported 0.0355–0.0984) but not the along-strike consistency constraint.

Non-fault mimic. Bedding-plane magnetic horizons and welded tuff contacts can also be tabular; dykes are genuinely sub-vertical and magnetic. Mitigation: require the depth estimate to be stable over ≥2 km of strike.

Data status. GeoDAWN DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ, public domain; the needed grids are already rasterised in geodawn_extensions_u8.tif (Th/K, U/K, U/Th, TMI_up150) and the 19-band stack. Verified present locally.

H12 · Paleo-geothermal deposit catchments (sinter / travertine / tufa) RANK 5 · partly tested · negative

Layers. GDR 1391 paleo-geothermal features compilation (709 mapped deposits), DOI 10.15121/1881483; rasterised as derived_gdr_paleo_100m_u8.tif.

Physical signature. A fossil spring deposit is the surface outlet of a conduit that is still there. The untried variant is not the along-strike continuation of the deposit (tested as VRC: HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0086 vs the parent's 0.0967 — strongly negative) but the upslope hydrologic catchment above it: the recharge area whose structurally controlled drainage delivered the fluid.

Why it catches a fault the catalogue misses. Deposits mark faults that were permeable in the Quaternary but have no preserved scarp at map scale — precisely the population the experts were asked to find.

Non-fault mimic. Deposits also form on purely lithologic springs and at springs controlled by the water table rather than structure. Mitigation: require the catchment to be bounded by a lineament on at least one side.

Data status. Free, official, CC BY 4.0, verified present locally — but the catchment variant needs a flow-direction computation on the 10 m DEM, which is the cost driver.
