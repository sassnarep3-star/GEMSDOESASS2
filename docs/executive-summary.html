The measured failure of proxy scoring. We built the largest calibration set this repository has had — 40 owner-scored rasters (9 SHA-pinned champions + 31 default-branch anchors) spanning 0.0107 to 0.2778 — and scored each against three measurable proxy truths (USGS SGMC geologic-map faults ≥200 m outside the catalogue, 62,649 cells; the same unbuffered, 82,151 cells; and the catalogue itself). Not one proxy predicts the reported score. Spearman 0.169 / 0.181 / 0.211 / 0.198 (all p ≥ 0.19); leave-one-out R² = −0.12 / −0.12 / −0.08 / −0.06 — all negative, i.e. worse than predicting the mean. Receipt: proxy_calibration.json. This is a negative result and it is the single most important finding of the session: no local instrument can currently rank two submissions for the hidden test set.

Stated plainly: on the only fault sets we can measure, the champion wins. Two honest readings, and we cannot distinguish them with local data:

  • Reading A (the champion is better). Its parent score surface places cells more efficiently than our gradient-boosted model, and our 0.23 estimate is optimistic.
  • Reading B (the instrument is wrong). The SGMC set is 95.5% disjoint from the Quaternary catalogue, and one owner submission that targeted it directly (gemsdoe29-sgmc-off-catalogue-44k) scored 0.0512 — far below random-level. SGMC faults may simply not resemble expert-identified new faults. If so, the −14% is noise.

Deviation from the pre-registered rule, disclosed. The rule was “maximise proxy-DTI subject to catalogue-DTI ≤ champion's and mass = 37,654.” The argmax was a0.0_sep6_c3 at 0.082206 (itself 13.18% behind the champion). We shipped a1.0_sep3_c2 at 0.08138 — 1.0% behind — because α turned out to be a no-op: the emission selects by top-K, and pα is monotone in p, so every α ≥ 0.5 returns the identical cell set. The argmax row is therefore not a model-driven placement at all. Flagged as IR-31-02.
