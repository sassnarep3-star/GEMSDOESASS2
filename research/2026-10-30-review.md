## 2. Why 0.2778 scored highest (what we can show, what we cannot)

**Measured (pixel-exact):** GEMSDOE32 (37,654 dots) equals GEMSDOE25 (44,090 dots) minus the 6,436 dots that lie within 2 px of the known catalogue. No GEMSDOE32 dot is outside GEMSDOE25. Receipt: `lineage_0_2778` in the gate receipt.

**Derived (checked numerically, 20,000 random trials, 0 violations):** for a ratio with α + β = 1, where TP, FP and FN are expectations, removing a dot with expected hit probability q raises DTI iff q < α·DTI. With α = 0.2 and DTI ≈ 0.26, the threshold is about 0.052. This is derived algebra, not a measurement, and it treats the distance-weighted max term as if it were additive. The identity is not in the repo's earlier notes (checked by grep 2026-10-30).

**Inference (USER-REPORTED scores):** the +0.0178 gain from 0.2600 to 0.2778 implies the removed dots had a mean new-fault hit rate below about 5.2%. That is the autopsy's "precision" story, now given a number. It is not a per-dot credit.

**Consequence for beating 0.2778:** keep any pixel whose calibrated probability of being within 300 m of an unmapped fault exceeds α·DTI (about 5.6% if DTI ≈ 0.28). Sparse dot sets are only optimal if the probability mass sits in the dots. We have no validated per-pixel posterior, so we cannot say whether a higher score is reachable with these data.
