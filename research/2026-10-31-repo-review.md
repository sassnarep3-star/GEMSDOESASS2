## GEMSDOE32 0.2778: measured facts vs explanation

**Measured on saved bytes in the repository:** GEMSDOE32 has 37,654 positive cells. The audit describes it as an exact parent/child relation to GEMSDOE25 after removing 6,436 cells within 2 pixels of the bridge catalogue; the 0.2600 and 0.2778 values attached to those files are not receipt-verified here. GEMSDOE31 is another catalogue-distance prune reported at 0.2708. The broader focus masks are highly overlapping, with GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 positive masks identical in the pairwise receipt. These results show closely related raster constructions, not independent methods.

**Official metric and task facts:** the DrivenData problem description says target faults were manually identified by experts as absent from the public USGS database; its DTI uses confidence/probability values, α=0.2, β=0.8 and a triangular kernel with 300 m support. Removing predictions near already-mapped faults could reduce false-positive weight *if* those predictions have low probability of matching hidden faults. But closeness to a mapped fault does not prove zero hidden-fault credit: splays, stepovers and mapping misalignment may be nearby. The metric is also non-additive at prediction-cell level because each truth pixel uses a maximum confidence-distance contribution and changes may affect both weighted TP/FN and FP terms.

**Judgment:** within this specific family, the association between fewer catalogue-adjacent dots and the highest user-reported value is plausible and consistent with the contest’s “new faults” target. It is not causal proof, not a measured per-dot precision effect, and not a leaderboard estimator. The previous README assertion that a 0.29 projection follows from a collar-only extrapolation is withdrawn. “Can we beat 0.2778?” **Unknown.** No validated experiment in this review supports “yes” or a projected score.
